# 2026-11-14: Planning poker tables

- Moved development and CI to Postgres 18, the same as production (#54).
- Planned the first game: a poker table for real cards, where the app is the chips and does the bookkeeping. No-Limit Texas Hold'em, 2 to 9 players, blinds and buy-ins per table, an optional turn timer, side pots, and a showdown everyone in the hand agrees on (decision 0030).
- Only the market's currency is played for. Betting store items might come later.
- Next: tables and seats (buy-in, top-up, leaving), then the hand engine with thorough tests, then the app screens.
- Built poker tables and seats: opening a table with its settings, sitting down with a buy-in, topping up to the maximum, leaving with the whole stack, closing an empty table. Leaving a market gets you up from its tables. The tests check after each step that every coin is accounted for.
- Built the hand engine: the poker rules on their own (blinds, turns, minimum raises, short all-ins, side pots, splits), and saving hands with the turn timer and the showdown agreement. A test plays thousands of random hands and checks no chip is ever lost or made.
- Built the poker screens: Games in the menu and on the market page, opening a table, and the table itself (seats and stacks, dealer prompts, your turn with a two-tap fold and raise shortcuts, the showdown agreement, sitting down, topping up and getting up).
- First test game on two phones. When a turn ran out, the other phone didn't show it was their turn: the server applied the timer but told nobody, and a phone whose clock runs a little ahead asked too early and never again. Now a run-out turn tells everyone, and the app tries again a few times after a deadline.
- From the test: sliders for the buy-in, topping up, and the new partial cash-out. Colored actions (fold red, check green, call blue, raise gold). Raising opens a slider with ¼, ⅓ and ½ pot and pot shortcuts, and an all-in button at its end that shines when picked.
- Second test game. The timer didn't restart between turns: loading a hand shared its action log with the saved copy, so the server never saw a new action. The next player got only the leftover seconds and was checked or folded at once, which looked like a skipped turn. Fixed, with a test. Added tests that the big blind still gets to check, raise or fold before the flop.
- With a timer, each round now waits until the dealer taps "Cards are out". The table is drawn round, with a seat for each possible player, a big timer ring in the middle, and you pick your seat by tapping it.
- Third round of polish: a colored label above each player with their last action (check, call, bet or raise, fold, all-in, and what they won), and folded players fade. Card outlines with a question mark show where the real cards are: the board in the middle and two by each player still in. The raise pop-up remembers the amount for that turn. Every poker slider has − and + buttons, which repeat when held.
- Added to the ideas notes: virtual cards as a table mode (real or virtual), and table skins.
- Slider fix: holding − or + repeated the step, which made a normal press jump too far. Now a tap is one step and holding does nothing extra (the slider is for big moves). Tapping a slider's amount lets you type one.
- Merged the poker screens (#58). Next round, the table gets livelier: tapping a player opens their page, your balance, chips at the table and the two together show above it, the cards are bigger and fanned above each player (so none hide behind another seat), and casino chips stack up in front of each bet. Animations: cards fly out from the middle when dealt, the board flips in, bets slide into the pot after each round, winnings slide to the winner, numbers count up, the player to act glows, folds fade out, and the last five seconds beat.
- From testing: dealing crashed the app. The card animation used React Native's easing, but Reanimated runs it on the UI thread and needs its own. Fixed. The game also needed scrolling: the table is now at most about half the screen, and during a hand the page shows only the table, the balances and your turn (the blinds line, the hand heading and the seat buttons come back once the hand ends).
